# Minna no Nihongo – Lesson 22 Grammar

## 1. Relative Clauses (Noun Modification)

In Japanese, a sentence in the plain form can be placed before a noun to describe it.

**Structure:**

Plain-form sentence + Noun

**Examples:**

- これは私が買った本です。
  - これは わたしが かった ほんです。
  - This is the book that I bought.

- あそこにいる人は田中さんです。
  - あそこに いる ひとは たなかさんです。
  - The person who is over there is Mr. Tanaka.

## 2. Describing People and Things

A clause before a noun gives additional information about that person or thing.

**Examples:**

- 私が住んでいる町
  - わたしが すんでいる まち
  - The town where I live.

- 母が作った料理
  - ははが つくった りょうり
  - The food my mother made.

- 日本で買ったカメラ
  - にほんで かった カメラ
  - The camera I bought in Japan.

## 3. Relative Clauses with Different Verb Forms

The clause before a noun can use different plain verb forms.

**Examples:**

- 昨日買った服
  - きのう かった ふく
  - Clothes I bought yesterday.

- 毎日読む新聞
  - まいにち よむ しんぶん
  - The newspaper I read every day.

- まだ食べていない料理
  - まだ たべていない りょうり
  - Food that has not been eaten yet.

## 4. Important Grammar Points

- The modifying sentence comes before the noun.
- Use plain forms, not polite forms ending in です or ます, inside the relative clause.
- Japanese does not use a separate word like "who", "which", or "that" in these clauses.
- The noun being described comes at the end.
